## 3. Constitutional engineering law

Physical objects exist before calculations. Geometry states where objects and conductors exist. Topology states what is connected. Routing materialises topology as explicit positive and negative paths. Physics consumes those paths. Evidence qualifies every input and conclusion. Standards validate named cases without replacing physical reality. Deterministic receipts bind inputs, methods, evidence and outputs. The Python package owns authoritative engineering. The browser collects intent and projects returned authority.

Geometry is both the physical and moral anchor. A route requires endpoints. A length must arise from a route or a declared installation allowance. Same-string pole identity must survive every segment. Factory-fitted conductor, field-installed conductor, installed slack and procurement allowance must remain distinguishable. A visually persuasive interface may not promote an assumption into engineering truth.

Evidence classes state provenance, not confidence alone. Manufacturer-declared, measured, project-declared, standards-derived, calculated, assumed, candidate and unresolved values are different kinds of claim. Precision does not promote authority. An MPPT-shaped fixture identifier does not establish equipment connectivity. A block diagram narrows an unknown only to the extent that it actually shows the internal relationship.

Every material observation must declare its boundary. A shallow history, capped search, first API page, truncated log, simplified geometry, temperature case, frequency range or model envelope is not the whole merely because the tool returned successfully.

## 5. Manufacturer evidence now available

The public inverter datasheet establishes a 1500 Vdc transformerless string inverter with twelve independent MPPT stages in the standard configuration, a maximum of two external input connectors per MPPT, a stated forty-ampere PV input-current limit per MPPT, a sixty-ampere short-circuit-current limit per MPPT, a 500 V to 1500 V MPPT range, a nominal PV input voltage of 1080 V and a maximum PV input voltage of 1500 V.

Its published block diagram shows current monitoring, DC switching, DC surge protection and a DC electromagnetic-interference stage before twelve MPPT stages, followed by a shared DC bus after those stages. This supports twelve separate MPPT processing stages and a common bus downstream of them. It does not establish the exact internal relationship between the two external connectors associated with one MPPT, whether they are hard-paralleled at entry, whether blocking exists or the abnormal-condition backfeed path. Published reverse-connection protection addresses incorrect polarity and must not be presented as reverse-current blocking.

The public bifacial dual-glass module datasheet establishes, for the 660 W bin, 38.1 V and 17.35 A at maximum power, 45.9 V open circuit and 18.45 A short circuit. It establishes a 1500 V maximum system voltage, a 35 A maximum series-fuse rating, dimensions of 2384 mm by 1303 mm by 33 mm, two-millimetre front and rear glass, 4 mm² factory conductors and asymmetric portrait lead lengths of 350 mm and 280 mm. The exact module model and revision-controlled source must be retained in the future equipment profile.

Three derived results must become explicit model cases rather than prose observations.

Thirty 660 W modules produce 1377 V open circuit at standard test conditions. Applying the published minus 0.25 per cent per degree Celsius open-circuit-voltage coefficient across an illustrative thirty-five-degree reduction from 25°C gives an 8.75 per cent increase and approximately 1497.5 V. This is close to the 1500 V module and inverter ceiling. Different published power bins produce different answers, and tolerance treatment can move the result beyond the ceiling. Module profile, power bin, source revision, temperature case and tolerance policy must therefore bind below whole-project level, at least by string group and where required by individual string.

Two 660 W strings produce 36.9 A using the published front-side short-circuit current. The published five per cent rear-gain case produces 38.74 A for two strings. The published ten per cent case produces 40.6 A and exceeds the stated forty-ampere per-MPPT input-current limit. Rear-side gain is therefore an electrical input affecting current headroom, not only an energy-yield quantity.

The two factory leads differ by seventy millimetres per module. This is 2.1 metres of pole-length asymmetry across one thirty-module string and 50.4 metres across twenty-four strings. Positive and negative factory-lead lengths must be stored independently. A symmetric factory-conductor model is already wrong before field conductor is installed.

## 6. Field geometry and physics boundary

First-hand photographs show factory-lead slack formed into coils and cable-tied to rear rails close to the rear glass of bifacial dual-glass modules. The photographs also show wet ground and conductive supporting structure. A module nameplate agrees with the public 660 W datasheet values, strengthening the identification of that observed module relative to either source alone.

The coiled slack is the defining field observation. A coil has enclosed area and inductance that a straight run of the same conductor length does not possess. It sits adjacent to a potentially capacitive surface. Its surge-relevant geometry was created during installation and is not represented by nominal cable length, a schematic or a conventional cable schedule. Declared slack quantity, slack geometry, attachment position and evidence source must become first-class route information where known.

A parallel-plate calculation using the module area, two-millimetre glass and an assumed relative permittivity near seven gives an upper bound of roughly 96 nF per module. This is an assumed bound, not a measurement. Dry effective earth-referenced coupling may be far lower. Wet conductive films and frame relationships may move coupling towards the bound. The model must therefore support environmental states and uncertainty rather than one universal capacitance constant.

A thirty-module portrait row at 1.303 m pitch is approximately thirty-nine metres before the external home run. At frequency content near one megahertz, an assumed cable propagation velocity near 1.8 × 10^8 m/s gives a wavelength near 180 metres and a quarter wavelength near forty-five metres. This is an applicability boundary: conductor paths on this scale may require propagation, reflection and impedance-discontinuity treatment rather than a single lumped element. It is not a claim about a particular transient or installation.
