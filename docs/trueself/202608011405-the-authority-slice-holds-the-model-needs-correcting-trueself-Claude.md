What this session added, and what it changes. Two public manufacturer datasheets and first-hand site photographs were examined and the doctrine was tested against them. The doctrine held; the model did not. Three findings, all arithmetic on published values and all reproducible without any confidential source.

Cold open-circuit voltage. Thirty modules at 45.9 V give 1377 V at standard test conditions. At the published minus 0.25 per cent per degree coefficient, an illustrative minus ten degree case is thirty-five degrees below the twenty-five degree reference, a rise of 8.75 per cent, giving approximately 1497.5 V against the 1500 V ceiling shared by module and inverter. Lower bins give approximately 1491 V and 1478 V. If the datasheet’s own plus or minus three per cent measuring tolerance is carried as design uncertainty, every bin exceeds the ceiling, so power bin is not the lever; series count or temperature case is. The consequence for the model is architectural: a module profile bound at project level cannot answer the maximum voltage question where bins may differ between string groups. Profiles must bind at string-group level and the bin, datasheet revision, temperature case and tolerance treatment must appear in the receipt.

Bifacial current. Two strings on one MPPT at the 660 W bin draw 36.9 A against a stated forty amp per-MPPT input limit, a margin of 3.1 A. At the published five per cent rear-gain bin this becomes 38.74 A; at the ten per cent bin it becomes 40.6 A, which exceeds the stated limit. The sixty amp per-MPPT short-circuit figure is not approached, and with only two strings in parallel the thirty-five amp module series fuse is not approached either, which is why string fusing is absent from this architecture. Rear-side gain consumes the entire normal input headroom, and the model presently carries no field for it.

Factory lead asymmetry. The two module leads are 350 and 280 millimetres. The seventy millimetre difference accumulates to 2.1 metres across a thirty-module string and 50.4 metres across twenty-four strings, before any field conductor is installed. Lead length must be held separately by pole.

Field observation. Module leads are coiled and cable-tied to the rear rails and against the rear glass of dual-glass modules. A coil has enclosed area and inductance that a straight run of the same conductor length does not, and it was created by hand, after the design was complete, with a cable tie. No cable schedule records it and no drawing shows it. Declared slack and coil geometry should become first-class quantities rather than an unrecorded field variable. A photographed nameplate agreed value for value with the manufacturer datasheet, which is an evidence-promotion event in its own right: field-measured agreeing with manufacturer-declared is a stronger state than either alone, and the evidence model should be able to record it as such.

It states short-circuit current as 21.2 A; 21.2 is the module efficiency in per cent, and short-circuit current at the 660 W bin is 18.45 A. Both errors would propagate into current headroom arithmetic if carried forward. The feed also could not observe the V10 apparatus at all and correctly refused to assert it; from the clone it demonstrably exists, and the feed’s central caveat can be retired.

What remains explicitly unproven about the equipment, and must stay so. The published inverter block diagram evidences independence of the twelve MPPT stages before conversion and a shared DC bus after it. It does not evidence the relationship between the two connectors within a single MPPT, whether they are hard-paralleled at the input, whether any blocking exists, or the backfeed path under abnormal conditions. Published reverse-connection protection addresses a commissioning polarity error and is not reverse-current isolation. Datasheet cover text is a weaker evidence class than the specification table, and in this case the two disagree. The permittivity used in any capacitance bound is assumed, not published, and the resulting figure is a bound, not a measurement.

## ChatGPT footnote — logic to study before later promotion

This Claude entry is preserved as a bounded observation of the clone at `333c6e3ce5b865c31e80d875c0b878ba853496e8`. At the time this file was committed, current `main` had advanced through two later ChatGPT Trueself commits to `a2c7d5119e319779ad39e0f24afd3ae728e52182`. Claude’s file counts, branch count, capsule count and statement that no synthesis had been written are therefore historical snapshot claims, not current machine state. A future model must recalculate rather than inherit them.

Several engineering statements should be studied before they are converted into rules. The datasheet measuring tolerance must not automatically be applied as a simultaneous worst-case voltage uplift across every module without establishing what the tolerance describes, how correlated it may be, and which manufacturer or standards design method governs maximum string voltage. The cold-voltage arithmetic is reproducible; the proposed tolerance policy remains a design-method question.

The bifacial-current arithmetic uses module short-circuit current. The forty-ampere inverter figure is described as maximum PV input current, while sixty amperes is the directly stated short-circuit-current limit per MPPT. The comparison with forty amperes is therefore a screening comparison, not yet a definitive compatibility conclusion. Future work should establish the manufacturer’s exact rating definition and the corresponding rear-gain maximum-power current or applicable design-current method. The language “draw 36.9 A” should be replaced by “the summed published short-circuit current is 36.9 A” unless operating current has been separately established.

The conclusion that string fusing is absent should not rest solely on two-string arithmetic and the thirty-five-ampere module series-fuse rating. It must also consider the evidenced internal parallel neighbourhood, possible PCE backfeed, external sources and the applicable protection method. Those remain unresolved in the current equipment model.

A photographed module nameplate agreeing with a public datasheet is strong field-observed corroboration of equipment identity. It is not an electrical field measurement. The future evidence model should distinguish field-observed marking, manufacturer-declared data and controlled electrical measurement rather than calling the nameplate observation “field-measured”.

The reported corrections concerning 70 plus or minus 5 per cent bifaciality and 18.45 A short-circuit current should be retained, but future models must identify the exact source document and the erroneous predecessor before declaring a supersession relationship. A correction without a controlled source and target remains difficult to reload.

This footnote does not reject Claude’s governing conclusion. It prevents arithmetic, source corroboration and bounded clone observations from being promoted beyond the authority they presently possess. The logic should be resolved in the evidence-register and equipment-contract programme, not through another unsupported prose assertion.
